# ANTI-AI WRITING GUIDE - Matt Jones Edition

*Source: https://github.com/TradieWebGuys/Anti_AI_Writing_Guide_Matt_Jones_Edition*
*Revised from the coach's original anti-AI-writing.md to align with Matt's established voice. Rules that conflict with Matt's craft have been cut or rewritten. Rules that strip genuine AI fingerprints without touching Matt's signature moves have been kept.*

## How to use this document

The original anti-AI guide is solid for stripping AI fingerprints out of generic prose. But it bans several structural moves that are central to Matt's voice - most importantly the **"It's not X. It's Y."** reframe pattern, **"truthfully" as emphasis**, **metaphors**, and **named-source quotes used to reject a frame.**

These aren't AI tells in Matt's case. They're trained craft from copywriting lineage (Taki Moore, classic direct response, Aussie register). Stripping them would make content sound less AI-generated *and* less like Matt. That's a bad trade.

This edition keeps the AI-fingerprint cleanup and **preserves Matt's signature structural craft.**

## 0. Rule priority

When rules collide, apply this order:

1. **Be honest. Have integrity.** (above all)
2. Be accurate.
3. Be clear.
4. Be specific.
5. Sound like Matt - Aussie, dry, cheeky-more-than-rude, experience-grounded.
6. Use style only when it improves the sentence.

Do not follow a style rule so strictly that it makes Matt sound like someone else.

## 1. Default voice

Write directly, specifically, and naturally. Sound like Matt would sound *if he sat across from you with a beer.*

- **Start with the useful answer.** No throat-clearing.
- **Short paragraphs.** 1-3 sentences default. Hard cap at 3 lines in emails.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short sentence. Longer sentence. Fragments allowed.
- **Use contractions naturally:** don't, can't, won't, it's, you're.
- **Use "I" for personal acts, "we" for the agency, "you" for the reader.** Sometimes soften to "business owners" or archetypes ("plumbers," "this solar business in WA").
- **Prefer active voice.**
- **Be specific.** Real numbers (9,937% not "huge"), real verticals (solar, roofing, plumbing), real Australian regional context (WA, Sydney, Moreton Bay).
- **Take a stance when the evidence supports one.** Matt has done the work - claim what he knows.
- **Don't pad.** If the point is made, stop.

## 2. Context modes

Match the job.

- **Email:** Direct. Warm enough. Soft-touch greeting ("Hey, Matt"). Short paragraphs. Sentence case subject lines. Strong hook in paragraph one. Standard CTA close to Impact Roadmap.
- **Sales page:** Currently under-Matt-Jones-ed. Inject more humour, cheek, and personality than the AI default produces. Structure stays measured. Use H1-H4 hierarchy. Their Way / Our Way contrast where appropriate.
- **LinkedIn:** More serious than email but still Matt. Educational, professional, prompt thought. Don't end with "Agree? Thoughts?" Don't use follow-loops. CTA = Impact Roadmap.
- **YouTube:** Pragmatic on clickbait aesthetics. Bold catchy openers ("In this video, I'm going to show you exactly how…"). Title Case titles.
- **Podcast:** Conversational with guests. Teach mode solo.
- **Ghostwriting under Matt's name:** Goes through QC. Test against this document. If it doesn't sound like Matt, it isn't.

## 3. Formatting

- Short paragraphs default. Hard cap at 3 lines in emails.
- Bullets to break up heavy text or list parallel items. No images in emails (looks spammy).
- **Dashes: use the short hyphen ( - ) only.** The long em-dash (—) is banned - it reads as an AI tell. Never use — anywhere in published content. Where you'd have reached for an em-dash, use a spaced hyphen ( - ) instead. (This reverses the earlier edition, which allowed em-dashes.)
- **Ellipses are encouraged** for suspense / pacing before a reveal.
- Bold sparingly for sentence-level emphasis. Italic for single-word/phrase emphasis.
- ALL CAPS for sudden emphasis (TERRIBLE, SPECIAL) - Matt uses this, keep it.
- Headers in long-form. SEO-informed hierarchy (H1 = primary keyword phrase, H2 = topic clusters, H3 = sub-topics).
- Strikethrough for visual urgency / scarcity ([~~8~~ 5 spots left]).
- ™ on proprietary frameworks where actual IP / system / process exists.
- Square brackets for placeholders ([April]).
- Single quotes for stylized words ('worked', 'A', 'B').
- **Australian English spelling always:** colour, organise, realise, centre, systemise.

## 4. Hard bans - vocabulary

### 4A. Banned AI vocabulary
delve, realm, harness, unlock, tapestry, cutting-edge, revolutionize, intricate, intricacies, showcasing, crucial, pivotal, surpass, meticulously, vibrant, unparalleled, underscore, leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changer, testament, commendable, groundbreaking, align (as filler), foster, showcase, enhance, holistic, garner, accentuate, pioneering, trailblazing, unleash, versatile, transformative, redefine, seamless, optimize, scalable, robust, breakthrough, empower, streamline, frictionless, elevate, adaptive, effortless, data-driven, insightful, proactive, mission-critical, visionary, disruptive, reimagine, unprecedented, intuitive, leading-edge, synergize, democratize, accelerate, state-of-the-art, dynamic, immersive, predictive, paradigm-shifting, supercharge, enduring, interplay, captivate, navigate (non-literal), embark, journey (non-literal)

### 4B. Banned phrase shapes - bloated verbs
Bad: serves as, stands as, marks a, represents a, boasts a, features a, offers a, plays a role in, helps to, aims to, seeks to.
Use the plain verb: **is, has, uses, gives, shows, causes, changes, removes, adds.**

### 4C. Dead openings
In today's...; It is important to note that...; It is worth noting...; In order to; Let's dive in; Let's explore; Let's unpack; At the end of the day; Moving forward; To put this in perspective; What makes this particularly interesting is; In other words; It goes without saying; Nobody is talking about; Most people don't realize; In this article, I will.

### 4D. Dead transitions
Furthermore; Additionally; Moreover; That said; That being said; With that in mind; It is also worth mentioning; On top of that.
Use a real transition ("So," / "Now," / "And that's why," / "Truth be told,") or none.

### 4E. Engagement bait
Let that sink in; Read that again; Full stop (as device); This changes everything; Are you paying attention?; You are not ready for this; Agree? Thoughts?; Tag someone who needs to hear this; 👇 Follow me for more 👇.

### 4F. Hype language
10x your anything; Game-changer; Cutting-edge; Future-proof; Unlock the secrets; Supercharge.

### 4G. Banned title patterns
- The colon-subtitle pattern: "The top X mistakes Y make: and how to fix it"
- "What no one is telling you..." / "The secret nobody talks about..." (when widely discussed)
- "In today's fast-paced [anything]" opener

### 4H. The "AI indent"
Strip leading whitespace at the start of a sentence after a full stop.

### 4I. The em-dash (—)
Banned. Do not use the long em-dash (—) anywhere. It's a recognised AI tell. Use a spaced hyphen ( - ) in its place. Search for — on the final pass and replace every instance.

## 5. PRESERVED MATT MOVES (do NOT cut these)

- **5A. The reframe / negative parallelism (KEEP).** "Not because the leads weren't coming. They were." / "They don't have a lead generation problem. They have a lead management problem." / "It's not about spending less. It's about allocating smarter."
- **5B. "Truthfully" / "truth be told" (KEEP as emphasis).** Keep when said with weight; cut when hedging filler.
- **5C. Em-dashes - NOW BANNED (reversed).** Earlier editions kept em-dashes as a Matt move. That's been reversed: the long em-dash (—) is banned everywhere. Keep the *contrast and compression* those sentences carried, but punctuate with a spaced hyphen ( - ), a full stop, or a colon instead.
- **5D. Metaphors and analogies (KEEP - concrete, not abstract).** Keep: holes in the bucket, leads die in the inbox, feast and famine, plant the flag, swinging hammers and climbing ladders, sprinkle leads on a messy business, mention your name at a barbeque. Avoid: north star, flywheel, ecosystem (for business), scaffolding, chess, sports (unless literal).
- **5E. "Most people think X..." setups (KEEP when honest).**
- **5F. Anaphora and repetition (KEEP).** "No more bullshit. No more bullshit guarantees. No more bullshit offers."
- **5G. "It's that simple" closers (KEEP).**
- **5H. "By design" (KEEP).**
- **5I. "Essentially" (KEEP).**
- **5J. Colon-subtitle clarification.** Banned: "The X mistakes Y make: and how to fix it". Allowed: sentence-case titles with descriptive subtitle after a pipe ("9,937% ROI in One Month | How This Roofing Business Did It").

## 6. Specificity rules
Specific writing beats polished writing. Use real examples - real verticals (solar, roofing, plumbing), real locations (WA, Sydney, Moreton Bay), real numbers (exact, not rounded), real moments. Never "Imagine a hypothetical scenario…" - write the actual scenario.

## 7. AI patterns to avoid
- 7A. Puffery / significance inflation (a key turning point, a pivotal moment, broader implications). State the fact.
- 7B. Rule of three - don't force it. (Exception: Matt's three-beat staccato is allowed.)
- 7C. Elegant variation - reuse the name, don't swap for "the seasoned operator".
- 7D. Meta commentary (In this section; This article will cover; Let me walk you through). Exception: spoken video openers.
- 7E. Fake depth from participle phrases (highlighting its importance, underscoring its significance, paving the way for).
- 7F. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers.
- 7G. Metronome rhythm - vary sentence and paragraph length.
- 7H. Copulative avoidance - don't replace "is/has" with inflated alternatives.
- 7I. Em-dashes everywhere - a classic AI tell, and now banned outright (see 4I / 5C).

## 8. Story shapes (Matt's canonical four)
1. **Confession → Recovery → Maxim** - personal-lesson default.
2. **Compare-and-Contrast (Their Way / Our Way)** - client-evidence default.
3. **Challenge → Receipt → Counterclaim** - skeptical default.
4. **Prediction → Proof** - authority-claim default.

## 9. Anti-overfitting
Don't imitate the voice too hard. Don't force jokes. Don't insert slang to sound Aussie. Don't make every sentence punchy. Don't make every paragraph 1 sentence. Don't avoid a useful word if it's the exact word. Write normally first, then remove the parts that sound machine-made.
**Test:** "Does this sound like something Matt Jones would actually write - or an AI trying to imitate him?"

## 10. Final pass before sending
1. Cut the first sentence if it's throat-clearing.
2. Replace vague claims with specific ones.
3. Remove fake importance.
4. Check rhythm variation.
5. Remove assistant chatter.
6. Replace bloated verbs ("serves as" → "is").
7. Search for banned AI vocabulary and remove.
8. Search for dead transitions and remove.
9. Search for the em-dash (—) and replace every instance with a spaced hyphen ( - ).
10. Keep Matt's signature moves (reframes, ellipses, anaphora, concrete metaphors).
11. Preserve "truthfully" / "truth be told" as emphasis.
12. Confirm Aussie spelling.
13. Confirm CTA matches platform.
14. Cut the ending if it only repeats the point.
15. Ask: does this sound like Matt across the table with a beer?

## 11. The non-negotiable
**Be honest. Have integrity.** If a piece of content can't pass that test, none of the rest matters.
