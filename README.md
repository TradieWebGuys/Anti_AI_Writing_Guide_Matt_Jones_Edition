# ANTI-AI WRITING GUIDE — Matt Jones Edition

*Revised from the coach's original anti-AI-writing.md to align with Matt's established voice. Rules that conflict with Matt's craft have been cut or rewritten. Rules that strip genuine AI fingerprints without touching Matt's signature moves have been kept.*

---

## How to use this document

The original anti-AI guide is solid for stripping AI fingerprints out of generic prose. But it bans several structural moves that are central to Matt's voice — most importantly the **"It's not X. It's Y."** reframe pattern, **em-dashes**, **"truthfully" as emphasis**, **metaphors**, and **named-source quotes used to reject a frame.**

These aren't AI tells in Matt's case. They're trained craft from copywriting lineage (Taki Moore, classic direct response, Aussie register). Stripping them would make content sound less AI-generated *and* less like Matt. That's a bad trade.

This edition keeps the AI-fingerprint cleanup and **preserves Matt's signature structural craft.**

---

## 0. Rule priority

When rules collide, apply this order:

1. **Be honest. Have integrity.** (above all)
2. Be accurate.
3. Be clear.
4. Be specific.
5. Sound like Matt — Aussie, dry, cheeky-more-than-rude, experience-grounded.
6. Use style only when it improves the sentence.

Do not follow a style rule so strictly that it makes Matt sound like someone else.

---

## 1. Default voice

Write directly, specifically, and naturally. Sound like Matt would sound *if he sat across from you with a beer.*

- **Start with the useful answer.** No throat-clearing.
- **Short paragraphs.** 1-3 sentences default. Hard cap at 3 lines in emails.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short sentence. Longer sentence. Fragments allowed.
- **Use contractions naturally:** don't, can't, won't, it's, you're.
- **Use "I" for personal acts, "we" for the agency, "you" for the reader.** Sometimes soften to "business owners" or archetypes ("plumbers," "this solar business in WA").
- **Prefer active voice.**
- **Be specific.** Real numbers (9,937% not "huge"), real verticals (solar, roofing, plumbing), real Australian regional context (WA, Sydney, Moreton Bay).
- **Take a stance when the evidence supports one.** Matt has done the work — claim what he knows.
- **Don't pad.** If the point is made, stop.

## 2. Context modes

Match the job.

### Email
Direct. Warm enough. Soft-touch greeting (*"Hey, Matt"*). Short paragraphs. Sentence case subject lines. Strong hook in paragraph one. Standard CTA close to Impact Roadmap.

### Sales page
*Currently under-Matt-Jones-ed. Inject more humour, cheek, and personality than the AI default produces.* Structure stays measured (this is a high-stakes asset) but voice stays Matt. Use H1-H4 hierarchy. Use Their Way / Our Way contrast where appropriate.

### LinkedIn
More serious than email but still Matt. Educational, professional, prompt thought. Don't end with "Agree? Thoughts?" Don't use follow-loops. CTA = Impact Roadmap.

### YouTube
Pragmatic on clickbait aesthetics — *"whatever results in better outcome."* Bold catchy openers ("In this video, I'm going to show you exactly how…"). Title Case titles.

### Podcast
Conversational with guests. Teach mode solo. Standard opening question: *"Can you tell the audience who you are, what you do, and a little bit about what we're going to be talking about today?"* Standard close: *"Where's the best place for people to find them?"*

### Anything published under Matt's name (team ghostwriting)
Goes through QC. Test against this document. **If it doesn't sound like Matt, it isn't.**

## 3. Formatting

- **Short paragraphs default.** Hard cap at 3 lines in emails.
- **Bullets** to break up heavy text or list parallel items. No images in emails (looks spammy).
- **Em-dashes are allowed** (e.g., *"this is an opportunity for you—but also an obligation"*) — they're a Matt move, not an AI tell.
- **Ellipses are encouraged** for suspense / pacing before a reveal.
- **Bold sparingly** for sentence-level emphasis. Italic for single-word/phrase emphasis.
- **ALL CAPS** for sudden emphasis (TERRIBLE, SPECIAL) — Matt uses this, keep it.
- **Headers** in long-form. SEO-informed hierarchy where appropriate (H1 = primary keyword phrase, H2 = topic clusters, H3 = sub-topics).
- **Strikethrough** for visual urgency / scarcity ([~~8~~ 5 spots left]).
- **™** on proprietary frameworks where actual IP / system / process exists.
- **Square brackets** for placeholders ([April]).
- **Single quotes** for stylized words ('worked', 'A', 'B').
- **Australian English spelling** always: colour, organise, realise, centre, systemise.

## 4. Hard bans — vocabulary

Do not use these in published content. They are AI fingerprints, hype, or both.

### 4A. Banned AI vocabulary
delve, realm, harness, unlock, tapestry, cutting-edge, revolutionize, intricate, intricacies, showcasing, crucial, pivotal, surpass, meticulously, vibrant, unparalleled, underscore, leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changer, testament, commendable, groundbreaking, align (as filler), foster, showcase, enhance, holistic, garner, accentuate, pioneering, trailblazing, unleash, versatile, transformative, redefine, seamless, optimize, scalable, robust, breakthrough, empower, streamline, frictionless, elevate, adaptive, effortless, data-driven, insightful, proactive, mission-critical, visionary, disruptive, reimagine, unprecedented, intuitive, leading-edge, synergize, democratize, accelerate, state-of-the-art, dynamic, immersive, predictive, paradigm-shifting, supercharge, enduring, interplay, captivate, navigate (non-literal), embark, journey (non-literal)

### 4B. Banned phrase shapes — bloated verbs to dodge "is" or "has"

Bad:
- serves as
- stands as
- marks a
- represents a
- boasts a
- features a
- offers a
- plays a role in
- helps to
- aims to
- seeks to

Use the plain verb: **is, has, uses, gives, shows, causes, changes, removes, adds.**

### 4C. Dead openings

Do not use:
- In today's...
- It is important to note that...
- It is worth noting...
- In order to
- Let's dive in
- Let's explore
- Let's unpack
- At the end of the day
- Moving forward
- To put this in perspective
- What makes this particularly interesting is
- In other words
- It goes without saying
- Nobody is talking about
- Most people don't realize
- In this article, I will

### 4D. Dead transitions

Do not use:
- Furthermore
- Additionally
- Moreover
- That said
- That being said
- With that in mind
- It is also worth mentioning
- On top of that

Use a real transition (*"So,"* / *"Now,"* / *"And that's why,"* / *"Truth be told,"*) or no transition at all.

### 4E. Engagement bait

Do not use:
- Let that sink in
- Read that again
- Full stop (as engagement device)
- This changes everything
- Are you paying attention?
- You are not ready for this
- Agree? Thoughts? (LinkedIn closer)
- Tag someone who needs to hear this
- 👇 Follow me for more 👇

### 4F. Hype language

No promises of superpowers, easy riches, or magic growth.

Do not use:
- 10x your anything
- Game-changer
- Cutting-edge
- Future-proof
- Unlock the secrets
- Supercharge

### 4G. Banned title patterns

- The colon-subtitle pattern: *"The top X mistakes Y make: and how to fix it"*
- *"What no one is telling you..."* / *"The secret nobody talks about..."* (when the topic is widely discussed)
- *"In today's fast-paced [anything]"* opener

### 4H. The "AI indent"

Strip leading whitespace at the start of a sentence after a full stop. (AI v1 sometimes inserts this; it's a tell.)

## 5. PRESERVED MATT MOVES (do NOT cut these)

These are signature craft, not AI fingerprints. The original guide banned them. This edition restores them.

### 5A. The reframe / negative parallelism (KEEP)

Matt's signature move. Examples from his actual writing:

- *"Not because the leads weren't coming. They were."*
- *"They don't have a lead generation problem. They have a lead management problem."*
- *"It's not about spending less. It's about allocating smarter."*
- *"Marketing won't fix a slow business."* (rejected frame implied)
- *"This isn't a conversation about what you can afford to spend on ads. This is a conversation about us helping you get to the target."*
- *"Same vertical. Same opportunity. Completely different outcome."*

**Use freely.** This is craft from direct-response copywriting and it works.

### 5B. "Truthfully" / "truth be told" (KEEP as emphasis)

When used as emphasis or honesty marker — keep.
When used as filler ("truthfully, I think...") — cut.

The test: would Matt *say* this word with weight, or is it just hedging? If weight, keep.

### 5C. Em-dashes (KEEP)

Matt uses them for compression and contrast:
- *"This is an opportunity for you—but also an obligation"*
- *"If you're running ads, paying for leads, and watching them die in the inbox between your office and your sales rep — that's the gap."*

Allowed and encouraged.

### 5D. Metaphors and analogies (KEEP — Matt's are visceral, not abstract)

Matt's metaphors are concrete physical images for his trade audience. They're translation devices, not decoration.

Keep:
- *Holes in the bucket* / *the bucket leaks*
- *Leads die in the inbox*
- *Feast and famine cycle*
- *Plant the flag*
- *Swinging hammers and climbing ladders*
- *Sprinkle leads on a messy business*
- *Mention your name at a barbeque*

**Test before using a new metaphor:** is it concrete (physical, sensory) or abstract (corporate, vague)? Concrete = keep. Abstract = cut.

Avoid: north star metaphors, flywheel metaphors, ecosystem metaphors *for business*, scaffolding metaphors, chess metaphors, sports metaphors (unless literal).

### 5E. "Most people think X..." setups (KEEP when honest)

The coach's guide banned these as reframe scaffolding. Matt uses them genuinely:

- *"Most tradies would have lost this $280k deal"*
- *"We hear all of the time people asking for these ridiculous guarantees..."*

Use when the setup is an honest observation about the audience, not a fake straw man.

### 5F. Anaphora and repetition (KEEP)

Matt's natural cadence. Use for conviction or comic escalation:

- *"No more bullshit. No more bullshit guarantees. No more bullshit offers."*
- *"I'll try peptides. I'll try anti-inflammatories. I'll try knee braces. I'll try massage. Heck, I'll even try a nudist camp."*

### 5G. "It's that simple" closers (KEEP)

Matt-signature close. Keep.

### 5H. "By design" (KEEP)

Matt's marker for deliberate structural choices. Keep.

### 5I. "Essentially" (KEEP)

Matt explicitly flagged this as one he uses and wants to preserve. Don't cut.

### 5J. The colon-subtitle title pattern — clarification

The title pattern Matt called out as AI (*"The X mistakes Y make: and how to fix it"*) is banned.

However, sentence-case titles with descriptive subtitles after a pipe (*"9,937% ROI in One Month | How This Roofing Business Did It"*) are Matt-standard and allowed.

## 6. Specificity rules

Specific writing beats polished writing.

Weak:
> *"The company faced challenges."*

Better:
> *"They had 22 leads, won zero, and spent $0 on the system that worked next door."*

Weak:
> *"The tool improves workflow."*

Better:
> *"DFY 5-min quotes instead of two days later when they got around to it."*

Weak:
> *"Users were frustrated."*

Better:
> *"Slow response times, partial tech adoption, quoting once a week on their schedule."*

**Use real examples.** Real verticals (solar, roofing, plumbing). Real locations (WA, Sydney, Moreton Bay). Real numbers (exact, not rounded). Real moments (the SMH/COVID interview, the ATO $100k bill, the WA solar client at $300k/month).

Do not write *"Imagine a hypothetical scenario..."* — write the actual scenario.

## 7. AI writing patterns to avoid

### 7A. Puffery and significance inflation

Don't inflate normal facts.

Avoid:
- a key turning point
- a pivotal moment
- a major shift
- setting the stage for
- marking a significant evolution
- broader implications

State the fact. Let the reader judge weight.

### 7B. Rule of three (don't force it)

Don't make every claim into 3 items. Use 1 if 1 matters. Use 2 or 4 if that's true.

**Exception: Matt's three-beat staccato** (*"Same vertical. Same opportunity. Completely different outcome."*) is a rhythmic device, not generic three-listing. It's allowed.

### 7C. Elegant variation

Don't swap names just to avoid repetition. Use the name again.

Bad:
> *"Sarah joined the company in 2021. The seasoned operator then led the team."*

Better:
> *"Sarah joined the company in 2021. She then led the team."*

### 7D. Meta commentary

Don't announce the writing.

Avoid:
- In this section
- This article will cover
- Let me walk you through
- Here is a comprehensive overview

Say the thing.

**Exception:** Matt's spoken video openers use this pattern naturally (*"In this video, I'm going to show you exactly how..."*). Spoken video ≠ written content. Allowed in video scripts.

### 7E. Fake depth from participle phrases

Avoid vague phrases that pretend to analyse:
- highlighting its importance
- underscoring its significance
- reflecting broader trends
- contributing to a rich history
- paving the way for
- opening the door to

If the analysis matters, give it its own sentence with a specific claim.

### 7F. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers

Do not include:
- As of my last update
- Based on available information
- While specific details are limited
- I do not have real-time access

If current facts matter, verify them.

### 7G. Metronome rhythm

Avoid same-length sentences and same-size paragraphs. Vary.

### 7H. Copulative avoidance

Don't replace "is" or "has" with inflated alternatives.

Bad:
> *"The report serves as a guide."*

Better:
> *"The report is a guide."*

## 8. Story shapes (Matt's canonical four)

Match the shape to the goal:

1. **Confession → Recovery → Maxim** — personal lesson default
   - *Use when: teaching from your own paid lesson*
2. **Compare-and-Contrast (Their Way / Our Way)** — client-evidence default
   - *Use when: showing the system delta with numbers*
3. **Challenge → Receipt → Counterclaim** — skeptical default
   - *Use when: dismantling a peer claim with experience*
4. **Prediction → Proof** — authority-claim default
   - *Use when: you have a real contrarian prediction that came true*

## 9. Anti-overfitting guide

This file describes Matt's taste. It doesn't replace judgment.

- Don't imitate the voice too hard.
- Don't force jokes.
- Don't insert slang to sound Aussie.
- Don't make every sentence punchy.
- Don't make every paragraph 1 sentence.
- Don't avoid a useful word if it's the exact word.
- Don't turn the output into a checklist of avoided mistakes.

Write normally first. Then remove the parts that sound machine-made.

**The test:**
> *"Does this sound like something Matt Jones would actually write — given his Aussie register, his experience-grounded authority, his picking-fights headline / teaching-body structure — or does it sound like an AI trying to imitate him?"*

If it feels forced, simplify it.

## 10. Final pass before sending

Run this pass silently:

1. **Cut the first sentence** if it's throat-clearing.
2. **Replace vague claims** with specific ones (real verticals, locations, numbers).
3. **Remove fake importance** (puffery, significance inflation).
4. **Check rhythm variation.** Mix short and long sentences.
5. **Remove assistant chatter** ("Let me walk you through...").
6. **Replace bloated verbs** ("serves as" → "is").
7. **Search for banned AI vocabulary** and remove.
8. **Search for dead transitions** (furthermore, moreover) and remove.
9. **Keep Matt's signature moves** — reframes, em-dashes, ellipses, anaphora, metaphors that pass the "concrete-not-abstract" test.
10. **Preserve "truthfully" / "truth be told"** when used for emphasis.
11. **Confirm Aussie spelling** (colour, organise, realise, systemise).
12. **Confirm CTA matches platform** (Impact Roadmap booking link for cold/warm conversion content).
13. **Cut the ending** if it only repeats the point.
14. **Ask: does this sound like Matt across the table with a beer?**

If yes, send. If no, fix it.

---

## 11. The non-negotiable

Above all rules, beneath all rules:

**Be honest. Have integrity.**

If a piece of content can't pass that test, none of the rest matters.
